καταστερισμός — a placing among the stars

The sieve that finds
them is his too.

Eratosthenes catalogued the constellations and invented the method for finding prime numbers. Twenty-two centuries later, a small C program runs his sieve on machines that would otherwise be idle, looking for constellations made of primes — and pays for the search in the coin it mines.

Six primes in fixed formation. The gaps never vary: 4, 2, 4, 2, 4. Find the first four and you have done enough work to claim a share; find all six and you have found a block. The smallest is 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23 — and it is the only one that does not obey the rule on the next plate.

Cyrene, c. 276 BCE — Alexandria, c. 194 BCE

A librarian who measured
the world with a shadow.

Eratosthenes ran the Library of Alexandria. His rivals nicknamed him Beta — second-best at everything, never first at one thing. The joke has not aged well. Three of the things he was second-best at are, between them, this entire project.

The Sieve

κόσκινον Ἐρατοσθένους

Write the numbers out. Strike every multiple of two, then of three, then of five. What is left has never been divided by anything. It is still the fastest way to find small primes, because there is nothing to improve.

Here: a wheel built from 2·3·5·7 eliminates 77% of candidates before a single primality test runs.

The Catasterismi

Καταστερισμοί

A catalogue of the constellations and the myths that put them there — a catasterism is the act of placing a figure permanently into the sky. The surviving text is likely a later epitome, but the work is his.

Here: the project's name, and its unit of work. Every accepted find is fixed in the chain the way a figure is fixed in the sky.

The Measurement

περὶ τῆς ἀναμετρήσεως τῆς γῆς

At noon on the solstice the sun stood directly over a well at Syene. At Alexandria, it did not — it cast a shadow of about 7.2°. From that angle and one road distance he computed the circumference of the Earth.

Here: the method. Small idle observations, carefully combined, answer a question far larger than any one of them.

Run his sieve.

Below are the numbers 1 to 210. Strike the multiples of 2, 3, 5 and 7 and what survives is not just a list of primes — it is the wheel this miner searches on. Every prime sextuplet above 7 sits at one specific residue in it.

Press run. The strikes follow Eratosthenes' order: smallest surviving number first.

What the node is
actually looking for.

A prime constellation is a set of primes in fixed relative positions. This one has six members and a span of sixteen. They are rare, they get rarer as the numbers grow, and there is no way to find one except to look.

The wheel does the eliminating

210 is 2·3·5·7. Only 48 of the 210 residues are coprime to it, and a sextuplet must land on exactly one of them: 97. Everything else is struck before any arithmetic happens.

Then it is just testing

What survives the wheel gets a Fermat test. About 42 million candidates a second on six cores at 448 bits — and almost all of them fail at the first member.

Work is proof, not promise

A share is a found quadruplet. Nobody has to be trusted: the number either has four primes in formation or it does not, and anyone can check in milliseconds.

It runs on the machine you have

One static binary, under a megabyte of memory, at idle scheduling priority — the next section is the whole budget.

Idle means idle

The entire cost
of running a node.

"Spare capacity" is easy to claim and rarely true — most of it arrives as a runtime, a daemon, a gigabyte of resident memory and a fan that never stops. These are measured figures from a node on a laptop, not targets.

141 KB
the whole binary

Statically linked against musl and stripped. ldd reports “not a dynamic executable” — there is no libc to install, no runtime, no interpreter. It is one file, and a build gate fails if it grows past 150 KB.

916 KB
resident memory

A running node with two mining threads, peaking at 1.5 MB. The sieve works in a 32 KB bitmap per worker; nothing else is large enough to matter.

SCHED_IDLE
and nice 19

Workers run at the kernel's lowest scheduling class. Anything else that wants the CPU takes it immediately — the miner is not competing with your machine, it is consuming what is left after everything else has finished.

82 °C
held, not hoped for

A controller samples the die at 10 Hz, medians the noise out, and drives duty cycle against a target. It gives back throughput rather than heat, and stops entirely if something else is already cooking the chip.

One detail for the people who will enjoy it: musl deliberately stubs the sched_setscheduler wrapper, so the throttle reaches for the raw syscall instead. That is the kind of thing you find out when you refuse to link against anything.

Thirty for the chain, seventy for the question

Most of the reward
never goes to mining.

Proof-of-work normally burns everything it earns on the proof. Here the proof is the smaller half of the job. The rest funds a second search whose results are useful whether or not anyone ever trades the coin.

30%

The consensus lane

Pays the miners who secure the chain, shared out by the work each one actually did over the last 256 shares.

70%

The science lane

Funds a search for prime gaps — long stretches of consecutive composite numbers, the emptiest reaches between primes. A claim is self-certifying: it names a gap, and any node can verify it with a bounded number of tests rather than repeating the search.

Claims are weighted by difficulty, not by length. A gap is exponentially harder to find as it grows, so the reward grows the same way — a merit-4 find earns hundreds of times a merit-2 one, not twice.

The escrow is a reservoir, not a hoard. Each block adds 35 coins and releases a tenth of the balance, so it climbs to 315 coins and stops. From there it pays out 35 a block — precisely its own inflow — to whoever is finding gaps. It cannot drain, and it cannot run away.

Run a node.

One binary, no daemon to configure, no wallet service to register with. It creates a key on first run and starts searching.

You can look at the live chain before installing anything. The explorer serves the same page two ways — HTML to a browser, plain text to a terminal:

# the live network, right now
curl explorer.catasterism.xyz

Then run your own:

# build and start a node
docker compose up --build -d

# watch it work
docker compose logs -f node1

# your own explorer, same dashboard
curl 127.0.0.1:3071

The explorer is a separate Go implementation that follows a node and re-derives the entire chain from scratch — the work, the linkage, the commitments, the balances. It never trusts the node's arithmetic. If the two ever disagree, the dashboard says so on its first line.
